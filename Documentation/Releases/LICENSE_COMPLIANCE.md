engineering record of the licences in the files, not legal advice.

## Confirmed rights and obligations

**Fonts (OFL 1.1).** The OFL allows the fonts to be bundled, embedded and redistributed with software,
provided:

- each copy carries the copyright notice and the licence;
- the fonts aren't sold on their own;
- a modified version is renamed.

Qeetrix bundles both fonts **unmodified**, each beside its licence (`Fonts/CalSans-OFL.txt`,
`Fonts/FiraCode-OFL.txt`), so every app that ships Qeetrix ships the licences inside its bundle. Neither
font declares a Reserved Font Name. The web's family name "Qeet Sans" is a CSS alias; the iOS file keeps
its original names.

**Icons (MIT, ISC).** MIT and ISC permit redistribution, provided the copyright and permission notices
are included in copies. As of Phase 7 the complete notice travels in the bundle
(`Licenses/qeetrix-icons-LICENSE.txt`), pinned by the lock. It is byte-identical to upstream's
`LICENSE`, which names Lucide's ISC licence and Feather's MIT licence; `IconTests` checks it. Until
Phase 7 it was only in the repository, so an app shipping the icons didn't carry it (fixed, P2).

**Test-only packages.** They are used to build and run tests only. An app that depends on Qeetrix
doesn't fetch them (`Scripts/consumer-check.sh` checks its `Package.resolved`). Their licences are
reviewed in `Tools/dependency-allowlist.json`.

The full list with versions, hashes and licences is the bill of materials,
`qeetrix-ios-0.1.0-rc.1.cdx.json`, generated by `Scripts/sbom.sh` from the pinned inputs.

## For apps that ship Qeetrix

- The licences are in the app's copy of `Qeetrix_Qeetrix.bundle`. That meets the "include the notice"
  terms, but listing them in the app's acknowledgements (Settings or About) is good practice. The
  texts are at `Fonts/CalSans-OFL.txt`, `Fonts/FiraCode-OFL.txt` and
  `Licenses/qeetrix-icons-LICENSE.txt` in that bundle.
- Don't extract the fonts for other uses without keeping their licences with them.
- The app's own privacy disclosures and App Store obligations remain the app's.

## Needs the owner's decision

1. **The licence of Qeetrix for iOS's own code — a release blocker for any distribution outside Qeet
   Group.** With no licence, the code is "all rights reserved". That is enough for Qeet Group's own apps
   built by people with access to the private repository, which is how `@qeetrix/ui` is treated
   (published as `UNLICENSED`). Choosing a licence — or confirming in writing that the library is
   proprietary and internal — is the owner's decision; this repository doesn't invent one.
2. **Who may receive it.** Today: anyone with read access to the private repository. Contractors or
   partners would need that access, and terms for it.
3. **A copyright notice.** The source files carry none. If the owner wants one (for example
   "© Qeet Group"), it should be added consistently, by decision.

None of these blocks internal use by Qeet Group apps, provided the owner confirms decision 1 as
internal and proprietary.
